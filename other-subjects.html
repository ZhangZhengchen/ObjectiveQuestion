Additional subject sets

Three sources. Ten focuses each.

For each source, we identified ten assessment focuses from the material listed below and generated one objective question per focus. Each source therefore has ten focuses and ten questions. Browse all thirty questions, with their reference answers and explanations.

These are research adaptations, not official course assessments. They are separate from the main AI-literacy and literary-analysis review. If you are taking part in that review, please finish it before browsing these examples.

What these examples illustrate

Single-choice and multiple-select questions can ask readers to judge explanations or teaching responses. Matching links observations with evidence or instructional functions. Process ordering preserves dependencies between actions. Ranking permits tied positions when alternatives provide equivalent support. Judgment matrices evaluate several claims separately.

The source collection includes two public courses and one teaching resource: CMU’s Learning Principles is a resource page, rather than a complete course. The examples illustrate selected units and targets; they do not establish full-course coverage, student difficulty or learning gains.

Researcher-designed adaptations. Original source and applicable Creative Commons licence are identified with each example. Teaching scenarios are fictional or source-inspired; no learner records are included.
