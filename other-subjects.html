Selected examples show how the framework can express educational analysis and teaching decisions through different objective response formats. Browse the assessment focus, question, reference answer and explanation.

These are curated research adaptations from additional public sources, not official course assessments or a complete evaluation set. They are separate from the main AI-literacy and literary-analysis review. If you are taking part in that review, please finish it before browsing these examples.

What these examples illustrate

Single-choice and multiple-select questions can ask readers to judge explanations or teaching responses. Matching links observations with evidence or instructional functions. Process ordering preserves dependencies between actions. Ranking permits tied positions when alternatives provide equivalent support. Judgment matrices evaluate several claims separately.

The source collection includes four public courses and one teaching resource: CMU’s Learning Principles is a resource page, rather than a complete course. The examples illustrate selected units and targets; they do not establish full-course coverage, student difficulty or learning gains.
