Five stages: from course relations to objective items

The assessment focus is a bounded learner judgment plus the evidence one item should elicit. It is more specific than a topic name or broad knowledge, skills and abilities inventory.

1. Select related concepts

Use bounded teaching sources and the curricular intent to select a minimal coherent concept set. Keep exact source evidence. There is no concept-count quota. A teacher checks selection and support.

2. Specify the concept relation

Identify a genuine distinction, conditional choice, dependency or other source-supported relationship. Ask what learners must discriminate or decide. Agreeing with an obviously desirable statement is insufficient for an application target. Preserve foundational recognition where the blueprint needs it.

3. Instantiate a scenario

Supply necessary case facts and constraints. The relationship must matter to the decision. Separate fictional case premises from taught claims; avoid decorative context and answer-revealing mappings.

4. Approve the assessment focus

Specify the observable judgment, required response evidence, supplied support and excluded requirements. Check consistency with the initial curricular intent. If the scenario cannot support the focus, revise upstream. Do not redefine the focus after seeing a generated item to make it look aligned.

5. Construct and inspect the item

Select the response format according to the evidence: matching for discriminable roles, multiple selection for parallel valid decisions, and single choice when a best-response judgment is intended. The primary study permits single choice, multiple selection, matching and judgment matrices. Ordering remains an extension outside this study.

Use plausible misconceptions, not obvious audience, format or source violations. Single choice uses four alternatives; multiple selection has at most five; judgment matrices at most four statements; matching at most four rows and five choices. Keep English learner text within 160 words and specify whole-item exact-match scoring and matching reuse.

Illustration: capabilities, workflows and human checks

An AI-generated competitor-price comparison contains inconsistent totals and may compare different products or dates. The intended judgment is to distinguish generation from product/date alignment and independently checked calculation, and decide where generation can safely contribute after those checks. A chatbot is an interface, not an alternative category to a functional capability. A workflow or agent is not automatically superior.

A suitable multiple-selection item could distinguish checking a table before drafting a recommendation from asking the same unchecked generated answer to certify itself. This description illustrates authoring logic; it is not a scored experimental item.

Relation to earlier work

Evidence-centered design motivates aligning intended claims, evidence and tasks: Newton et al. (2021). This course-specific authoring specification is not a claim to invent concept relations, scenario generation or ECD. The earlier open-task/decompose/retain-provide-exclude chain is an optional planning aid, not a required stage in this revised method.

Difficulty is not estimated, calibrated or controlled. Intended cognitive demand is a design judgment; its relation to student difficulty requires future learner studies.
