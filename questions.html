Study and authoring transparency

Two authoring approaches use the same targets within each course. The complete workflow uses teaching material and concept relations; the minimal approach receives only the target followed by “Please generate one objective question for this assessment target.” The same generator model and settings are used. The comparison concerns the complete authoring package, not an isolated relation field.

Expert judgments concern target alignment, answer/scoring validity and question design quality. Four experts are invited; comparative results are pending. Method labels and the item-to-method key are withheld from this release. The neutral display does not remove visible differences in response format.

Full-workflow AI prompt
Author exactly ONE high-quality objective assessment item for the supplied teacher-confirmed learning outcome in an introductory AI course. Use the source material and the teacher's concept relations as the knowledge boundary. The outcome may be broad: choose a coherent, worthwhile sample within it, and state the transferable judgment in targeted_judgment. Do not pretend one item establishes the whole outcome. Do not change the approved outcome.
Before producing the final item, work through concept selection, the relevant relationship, a meaningful decision boundary, an economical scenario, and what evidence a selected response could show. These are internal design steps, not a mandatory verbose output. Extract an assessable decision, not a merely desirable teaching statement. Do not invent unsupported conceptual distinctions or artificially increase complexity.
Use the strongest suitable item structure: distinguish parallel framework components with matching, multiple selection or diagnosis; use ordering only for a genuinely constrained dependency sequence; use single best response only when integrated judgment is the intended evidence. Do not force diversity or choose a format because it looks sophisticated. Permitted formats: single_choice, multiple_select, matching, ordering, true_false_matrix.
TACOS should test discrimination of component roles or targeted local revisions while keeping other requirements fixed; avoid a bundle with one obviously good prompt and three blatantly bad prompts. For capability selection distinguish input, processing and required output, not just task vocabulary. Workflow components are roles, not automatically six chronological steps. For agent and ethics items use meaningful conditions, evidence and permissions, not agreement with good conduct.
Make correct and incorrect alternatives comparable and plausible. Wrong alternatives should embody specific course-grounded misconceptions, such as a confused role, changed constraint, wrong dependency or unjustified inference. Avoid absolute-word cues, longer correct answers, tautological restatement, lexical one-to-one matching, moral giveaways, unrelated alternatives and factual speculation. A learner should need the course distinction to answer, not merely find the same words in the stem. Keep the assessed distinction as required learner knowledge; supply contextual facts, not definitions or a glossary revealing the tested mapping. Include enough facts to establish the key without hidden assumptions. State denominators if comparing rates or counts. Explain unavoidable domain-specific terms briefly without giving away assessed knowledge.
Prefer a short familiar workplace or academic case. English stem plus options and rows must total at most 160 whitespace words, preferably 90-140; concise wording rather than obscure terminology. Do not overload one item to cover every outcome detail. Single choice exactly four options; multiple-select at most five options, at least two correct and one incorrect; matching 2-4 rows and 3-5 choices with explicit reuse policy; true/false 2-4 statements; ordering 3-5 steps, include all defensible orders if dependencies allow alternatives. Never impose an unsupported unique order. All student-needed facts and instructions belong in question/options/rows. No free-text justification is required from learners.
Internally check answer validity, ambiguity, unintended clues and the shortest shortcut before returning your final draft within this single call. Return one final item, not candidates, scores or claims of measured difficulty. Provide a defensible answer and concise explanation of why each option/mapping is correct or incorrect.
